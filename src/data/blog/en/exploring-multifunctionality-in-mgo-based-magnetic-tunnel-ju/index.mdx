What if a tiny device could both sense magnetic fields and remember information like a brain? Scientists have now created nanoscale gadgets that combine two cutting-edge technologies—magnetic tunnel junctions and memristors—into one multifunctional device. This breakthrough could pave the way for smarter sensors and new kinds of computing inspired by how our brains work.

> **TL;DR**
> - Researchers demonstrated MgO-based magnetic tunnel junctions that simultaneously exhibit magnetoresistance for magnetic sensing and memristive switching for non-volatile memory.
> - By doping the oxide barrier, they reduced power consumption and showed that memristive switching can reversibly control the magnetic properties, enabling multifunctional, reprogrammable nanodevices.

*Note: this summary is based on a preprint — research shared ahead of formal peer review.*

Magnetic tunnel junctions (MTJs) and memristors are two promising nanotechnologies driving advances in sensing, data storage, and unconventional computing. MTJs rely on electron spin to detect magnetic fields with high sensitivity, while memristors mimic synapses by changing resistance based on electrical pulses, enabling memory and learning functions. Traditionally, these devices have been developed separately due to their differing physical mechanisms and materials. Integrating both functionalities into a single device has been challenging but could unlock new possibilities for multifunctional electronics.

The researchers fabricated cylindrical MTJs with magnesium oxide (MgO) insulating barriers sandwiched between magnetic layers. They applied a three-step annealing process to optimize magnetic properties, achieving linear magnetoresistance responses essential for sensors. Electrical pulsing was used to induce memristive switching by creating and disrupting conductive filaments in the oxide barrier, with careful control to avoid damaging the device. They also experimented with doping the MgO barrier with tantalum to reduce power consumption during switching. Electrical and magnetic measurements were performed to characterize the devices' dual functionalities and their interplay.

The devices exhibited a tunnel magnetoresistance ratio of about 45%, indicating strong magnetic sensing capability with a linear response to magnetic fields and no hysteresis. After an initial electroforming step, the devices showed stable, reversible memristive switching with resistance changes up to 2000%, far exceeding the magnetoresistance effect. The memristive switching was bipolar and non-volatile, retaining resistance states for over 24 hours under constant voltage stress. Modeling of the current-voltage behavior confirmed the tunneling nature of the high resistance state and ohmic conduction in the low resistance state. Importantly, memristive switching could reversibly suppress and restore the spintronic (magnetic) functionalities, demonstrating true multifunctionality. Doping the MgO barrier lowered the power needed for switching by 20%, suggesting improved energy efficiency.

This study marks an important step toward multifunctional nanoscale devices that combine magnetic sensing and memory-like behavior in a single platform. Such devices could enable reprogrammable sensor arrays that adapt their sensitivity and function on demand. Moreover, the ability to electrically control magnetic properties through memristive switching opens exciting avenues for neuromorphic computing architectures that mimic brain-like information processing. The demonstrated scalability and reduced power consumption further enhance the potential for practical applications in automotive sensors, medical devices, and beyond.

While promising, the devices currently exhibit some deviations from ideal behavior, such as reduced magnetoresistance ratios due to fabrication challenges. Further optimization of materials and processing is needed to fully realize the potential of these multifunctional junctions. Additionally, the complexity of integrating and controlling both effects simultaneously requires more research to ensure reliability and reproducibility in large-scale applications. Nonetheless, the results provide a credible foundation for future exploration of combined spintronic and memristive technologies.
